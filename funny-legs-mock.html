Trails (Big Scissor + Split Leap, your picks; click a card to jump)

All cosmetic: drawn only on screens, nothing new over the network. The number in each card's corner is how many smoke puffs are alive right now (the cost). Every puff is the same small pre-drawn soft image, so the game draws them all in one go.

Trail notes:
  • Low cost by design: a fixed pool of puffs per player (oldest reused, never more), spawned by distance the foot travels (not every frame), all drawn with one pre-made soft image (one batched draw on the GPU); ribbons are two short lines per foot; nothing is drawn for players off the screen; a "fewer effects" setting can halve the pool on weak phones.
  • Colour: smoke takes the arena's dust colour with a hint of the player's colour, so it reads as smoke, not as a shot, and stays darker than players and bullets (the look rules).
  • Same code path as today's dust: it would replace the L2 running puffs and grow the landing puff; take-off and landing already come from what's drawn, so the network sends nothing new.
Earlier picks: walk A (Big Scissor) + jump J1 (Split Leap) + the knees-out landing every card shows. They read clearly at game size, they're silly without being messy, and they stay close to how the legs already work (bigger swing, a pose at the top of the jump).
  • Hits follow the legs. Bullets hit the physics legs, not the drawing, so the real leg motors must make these poses (preview = reality). Purely drawn tricks (B's wobble lines, D's rubber bend) must stay small or a player would shoot a leg that isn't there.
  • Fair for everyone: wider legs are a slightly bigger target while walking and jumping; it's the same for every player, and I'll measure the leg hit rate in the sim before and after.
  • Speed and jump height stay the same: only the look of the legs changes; the sim checks walk speed and jump height don't move.
  • Hurt legs keep their own look: a stump hops and a limp leg drags as now; these styles are for whole legs.
